# Evaluation principles

These are small experiments, not a universal leaderboard. The goal is to make a specific claim easier to judge and a result easy to challenge.

1. **Name the claim.** Link to the original source and distinguish the author's claim from the narrower question being tested.
2. **Specify the comparison before running it.** State the baseline, inputs, procedure, scoring, and decision rule. Record deviations rather than editing them out.
3. **Keep conditions comparable.** Change one meaningful factor where practical. Record versions, model, prompts, environment, access to tools, dates, and costs when available.
4. **Publish the evidence.** Share inputs, raw outputs, scoring, and commands when possible. Explain omissions. Never publish credentials or private material.
5. **Count misses.** Record failures, unavailable tools, and ambiguous outcomes. Do not turn an unfinished run into a positive result.
6. **Separate observation from interpretation.** A small example can demonstrate a failure or a useful workflow; it rarely establishes general performance. State uncertainty and competing explanations.
7. **Make a practical judgment.** Report what worked, what it cost in time or money, and who might benefit. Avoid precision that the sample cannot support.

Experiments can be revised when a flaw is found. Preserve the original run and describe what changed before interpreting a new one.
